# US petshop website design inspiration

Research for [Research US petshop website design inspiration](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/14), under [Plan the fictional petshop showcase store](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/1).

Access date: **2026-10-26**. This investigation covers US-facing websites only. Its purpose is to expand the reference set for an original, pt-BR pet-products showcase for Brazilian independent petshop owners. None of the directions below is selected.

## What this adds

The [worldwide investigation](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/2) established broad retail patterns and included Chewy and Wild One. This follow-up adds eight different references: two large retailers, two home-oriented product brands, two coordinated-accessory specialists, a fashion-oriented brand, and a playful toy brand. The more useful addition is a comparison of **image composition and brand expression**, supported by four directly inspected official images and one public stylesheet.

The evidence favors combining a clear retail structure with a deliberate visual idea. That is a project-specific recommendation, not a measured claim about which retailer converts better.

## Evidence and limits

- **Page-content evidence:** first-party homepage, collection, product, support, or policy pages retrieved through web tools. This establishes the content and controls exposed in those sources, not their final rendered position.
- **Direct visual evidence:** four official image files were downloaded temporarily and inspected. Their composition, visible colors, lighting, and subjects can be described. They are not screenshots of the surrounding websites.
- **CSS evidence:** PetSmart's public HTML and linked stylesheet were retrieved directly. Font files and named color declarations can be identified; the resulting live cascade and screen appearance were not audited.
- **Unavailable evidence:** direct HTML requests to most other sites were rate-limited; Petco returned an access restriction. Extracted web content remained available. One maxbone image link contained an unresolved width placeholder and returned an error, so no visual claim relies on it.

No browser screenshot session, responsive viewport, account, native app, cart interaction, or checkout was exercised. The earlier session's rejected browser-launch action was not retried. Accordingly, exact grid column counts, sticky behavior, breakpoint rules, type scales, font usage outside the retrieved CSS, accessibility conformance, and animation behavior remain unverified. Extraction can include hidden menus, duplicate cards, or inactive templates; it is not a wireframe. Some BARK content includes older campaign material, so it is useful for structure and imagery rather than a reliable calendar of current promotions.

Images are linked as reference evidence. This report grants no permission to reuse them.

## Four visual references worth inspecting closely

### Diggs: a scene that gives the product a place in the home

The [official desktop hero image](https://www.diggs.pet/cdn/shop/files/evolv-matte-black-1d-hero-desktop-2400x1000_1.jpg?v=1791236200&width=1200) is a wide interior scene. A dog rests in a dark crate beside a sofa on the right; window light, a lamp, wood, and fabric create a warm evening atmosphere. The left portion contains comparatively quiet wall and floor space. These observations concern the bitmap, not the position of live site text.

**Interpretation for this project:** a hero can sell a recognizable situation—resting comfortably at home—while leaving room for useful copy. Plan the image and headline together. A narrow crop would risk losing either the dog/product or the quiet area, so mobile may require a separate composition.

The [Evolv product page](https://www.diggs.pet/products/evolv) complements emotional context with product views, size choices, measurement guidance, and feature information. The useful lesson is a division of jobs: a lifestyle image establishes the situation; detail imagery and information help someone choose. Avoid borrowing unverified safety or health claims.

### The Foggy Dog: the collection itself supplies the color

The [inspected collection image](https://www.thefoggydog.com/cdn/shop/collections/139281268822.jpg?v=1791008172&width=1000) arranges several patterned collars horizontally in a staggered stack against pale pink. Green/red fabrics, gold-colored hardware, small charms, and intentional open space make the related assortment legible. The color description belongs to this photo; it is not an assertion about the site's entire UI palette.

The [holiday collection](https://www.thefoggydog.com/collections/holiday-shop?filter.p.product_type=Collar+Walk+Set&filter.p.product_type=Harness+Walk+Set) connects product types through a common occasion. For the fictional brand, the transferable device is an original, coherent collection—not the specific seasonal prints, names, or US calendar.

**Interpretation:** let product design carry some personality, then give the UI enough restraint to preserve product recognition. A collection image needs a planned relationship between products, materials, and colors; unrelated stock packshots will not reproduce that coherence.

### Lucy & Co.: expressive material with a clear silhouette

The [inspected harness image](https://www.lucyand.co/cdn/shop/files/lucy-co-sunflower-denim-dog-harness.jpg?v=1788329060&width=533) shows a blue-denim harness with yellow floral motifs, pale checked straps, and visible hardware on white. The complete silhouette is centered and the print remains readable. It is useful reference for how a visually busy product can still be easy to inspect.

The [walking-gear collection](https://www.lucyand.co/collections/gear) exposes related harnesses, collars, leashes, and holders, with feature labels and availability states. Repeated extraction text does not prove repeated visible UI, and the number of rendered columns was not established.

**Interpretation:** standardize the product footprint and crop across cards. Keep collection/personality names subordinate to a familiar product type, price, and relevant options. If a shopper selects another print or color, the gallery and cart thumbnail should still depict the intended variant.

### BARK: one large playful image can carry the mood

The [inspected Licksters campaign image](https://bark.co/cdn/shop/files/2026-Licksters-HomeBanner-800x940.png?v=1785774193&width=800) places a dog holding a turquoise toy in the lower portion of a saturated yellow field. Small decorative marks and a dark angled lower area frame the subject; substantial open space remains above. This is direct visual evidence of a campaign's composition, not proof of BARK's global background colors or live heading treatment.

The [homepage](https://bark.co/) organizes offerings around different forms of play and customization. Its subscription flow is outside the necessary retail demo; the useful idea is an understandable activity or need.

**Interpretation:** one bold campaign moment can provide personality while shopping controls stay calm. The dog's expression, toy shape, background, and crop are doing coordinated work. A random pet portrait plus decorative UI would be a different result.

## Typography, color, hierarchy, and product presentation

PetSmart provides the strongest source-level typography evidence in this investigation. Its [linked stylesheet](https://www.petsmart.com/hcp/_next/static/chunks/1y2hqf7ouavb0.css) declares Euclid Circular B font resources and red/blue color ramps, including named values such as red-500 `#fe3744` and blue-500 `#206ef6`. Another [linked stylesheet](https://www.petsmart.com/hcp/_next/static/chunks/1mn0tcqssb4xo.css) declares Euclid Circular B and Proxima Nova font resources. These are **CSS declarations**, not confirmation that a particular heading on a particular screen uses them. They are also not a proposal to copy these fonts or values.

The useful inference is to define a small, intentional type hierarchy and distinguish stable functional colors from campaign imagery. The later DESIGN.md should name the chosen families, weights, sizes, line heights, and semantic roles rather than relying on “friendly” or “premium” as instructions.

For information hierarchy, PetSmart's homepage product entries expose product identity, variant counts, ratings, and price information. Petco's homepage demonstrates stable species/category navigation alongside temporary campaign content. Those are content-structure observations. Neither source establishes an ideal card density for this project.

Fable's [crate detail page](https://fablepets.com/products/crate) combines a product narrative with material, fit, care, and FAQ content. maxbone's [carrier collection](https://www.maxbone.com/collections/carrier-bags) and homepage demonstrate familiar product types alongside named collections. This offers two alternatives to filling every page with promotional tiles: explain a product's role, or organize a small related range.

**Recommendation for later prototyping:** compare one compact and one more spacious product-list treatment using the same fictional assortment. Keep enough card information to make an informed next click. Decide the density from actual content and mobile usability; do not prescribe a column count from this research.

## Strongest reference by design role

| Design role | Primary reference | What to borrow as a principle | What the later prototype must test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage lifestyle scene | Diggs hero | A believable setting and deliberate quiet area around a product in use. | Copy/image balance and a separate narrow-screen crop. |
| Collection storytelling | The Foggy Dog flat lay | Related products, consistent materials, and one coherent theme. | Whether the collection clarifies discovery without adding clutter. |
| Product-card imagery | Lucy & Co. harness | Full silhouette, predictable footprint, readable material/pattern. | Comparable scale across different products and faithful variants. |
| Memorable campaign moment | BARK campaign | A bold image with a specific activity and a clear focal subject. | Whether the playful moment supports the offer and leaves shopping readable. |

Fable supplies a useful narrative/detail reference; Petco and PetSmart supply retail-structure checks; maxbone supplies a collection-naming/fashion contrast. No single website needs to become the template for the entire showcase.

## Four synthesis directions for discussion

These are original proposals, not claims about complete verified website styles.

| Direction | Proposed system | Reference inputs | Risk to discuss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Everyday neighborhood store | Clear category-led shopping, readable sans typography, one approachable pet scene, practical product information. | Petco/PetSmart structure with a deliberately small assortment. | It could feel generic unless the brand and hero have a concrete point of view. |
| Pattern-and-personality boutique | Quiet UI surfaces, coordinated original accessories, collection-led discovery, consistent white-background cards. | The Foggy Dog and Lucy & Co.; maxbone's capsule-collection organization. | Producing multiple believable patterned variants may be expensive. |
| Home-and-care store | Spacious storytelling, materials and comfort, in-use domestic photography, concise suitability guidance. | Diggs' hero composition and Fable's problem/product narrative. | A dog-furniture emphasis could exclude everyday products or other species. |
| Play-and-discover store | One vivid campaign image, activity-based merchandising, expressive pet photography, restrained utility areas. | BARK's inspected campaign and play-style propositions. | Excessive decoration or subscription-like choices could distract from normal shopping. |

The directions are alternatives to test, not ingredients to combine indiscriminately. Use the same representative products and pt-BR copy when comparing them. A general petshop assortment may include cats or other species; most boutique references here are dog-focused, so catalog scope must determine the actual animal representation.

## Implications for the existing decision map

[Choose the fictional brand positioning and voice](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/5) should establish which customer situation and promise deserve the strongest expression. “Friendly” alone is insufficient; a store for everyday essentials and a coordinated-accessory boutique make different image and merchandising demands.

[Choose the visual direction through rough design alternatives](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/9) should compare a homepage, a representative listing/card, and a product detail at mobile and desktop widths. Evaluate brand recognition, readable prices/options, and visual continuity through shopping. Research cannot substitute for those rendered comparisons.

[Define the DESIGN.md specification and ownership](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/12) should record the selected type system, functional/campaign color roles, spacing and density rules, content hierarchy, card states, photo treatments, and responsive composition rules. Where a specific value remains undecided, keep that decision visible rather than calling this report a finished design system.

[Choose the image art direction and asset brief](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/10) should separate lifestyle hero masters, collection compositions, isolated packshots, in-use/detail views, and thumbnails. Specify focal points, quiet space, product scale, material/lighting consistency, variant correspondence, and mobile alternatives. This research identifies useful roles, not an approved image count or permission to copy assets.

No additional decision ticket is necessary from this investigation: these existing decisions already own the choices it exposes. App implementation, final DESIGN.md, brand selection, and production-image work remain outside this research.
