# Worldwide petshop storefront inspiration

Research for [Research worldwide petshop storefronts and shopping patterns](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/2), under [Plan the fictional petshop showcase store](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/1).

Access date: **2026-10-26**. This is decision support for an original fictional, pt-BR store aimed at Brazilian independent petshop owners. No brand, page structure, visual system, or optional feature is approved by this report.

## Evidence and limits

The comparison uses ten first-party storefronts across Brazil, North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific. Page retrieval exposes navigation, product information, controls, and image references; it does **not** demonstrate that every exposed state is visible or every interaction works. Retrieved pages may reflect indexed/cached content, localization, or different merchandising variants.

Two first-party product photographs were downloaded temporarily and visually inspected: a Wild One harness and a Cloud7 bed with a dog. They are reference evidence only, are not committed here, and are not approved for reuse. Most storefront pages were examined as extracted content rather than rendered screenshots. A headless Chrome screenshot attempt was rejected by execution policy and was not retried.

No real checkout, authenticated account, native app, or interactive mobile viewport was exercised. App observations below are explicitly publisher descriptions. Consequently, this report makes no claims about current responsive breakpoints, exact site colors/fonts, mobile sticky controls, animation quality, accessibility conformance, conversion performance, or whether a cart is a drawer rather than a page.

## Reference detail

### Petlove

The [dry-food listing](https://www.petlove.com.br/cachorro/racoes/racao-seca) exposes filters for such attributes as age/size, price, special diets, offers, and reviews. Product entries include package quantity, multiple options, ratings, prices, and membership-related price distinctions. The lesson is that a credible consumables catalog needs structured attributes, not just a title and photograph.

For this showcase, start the catalog decision by asking which attributes actually change a shopper's choice. Multiple simultaneous price regimes would require additional explanation and simulated behavior; their existence in a large retailer is not a reason to add them.

### Petz

The [collars, leads, and harnesses listing](https://www.petz.com.br/cachorro/coleiras-guias-e-peitorais) exposes category, price, brand, pet, size, age, color, and functional-characteristic facets. This offers a useful contrast to food: accessories have different comparison needs. Generic filters repeated across every category would feel less credible.

The retrieved markup also contains unrelated payment-processing and contact content. That is **not** evidence that entering a category starts checkout, nor proof of the rendered page hierarchy. Use the facet vocabulary as research input, not the extraction order as a wireframe.

### Chewy

The [leads/collars category](https://www.chewy.com/b/dog-leashes-and-collars-344) includes sorting/filter controls and product entries carrying rating counts, prices, availability/delivery messages, and purchase or personalization actions. Some entries describe recurring-purchase prices or promotions. The useful distinction is between a standard product and an item that needs configuration before purchase.

A fictional store can demonstrate that distinction with a small number of deliberate variants. Delivery dates, review counts, and discounts should be consistent fictional content, rather than copied commercial promises.

### Wild One

The [harness collection](https://wildone.com/collections/dog-harnesses) exposes sorting/filter controls, color choices, product benefits, and coordinated kits with explicit contents. Its [cushioned-harness product page](https://wildone.com/products/cushioned-dog-harness) provides color/size selection, measurement information, care details, FAQs, and comparison material.

This is a strong reference for a product detail page that resolves uncertainty about fit. A coherent fictional product family could show how one color/size selection affects its matching image and cart line. The inspected [step-in harness photograph](https://wildone.com/cdn/shop/files/WO_VM_StepInHarness_Air_LimeadeReflective_PDP_04_4x5_Web.jpg?v=1784313778&width=900) shows a single bright lime product on a light neutral background, with hardware and construction legible. That is an observation about this photograph, not the site's palette.

### Pets at Home

The [harness listing](https://www.petsathome.com/product/listing/dog/dog-collars-harnesses-leads-and-tags/dog-harnesses) exposes filters, sorting, result counts, and product entries with variant ranges, colors, prices, and purchase actions. The homepage's life-stage and size pathways show ways to guide shoppers who think about their pet before thinking about a specific brand.

This suggests testing a shopper scenario such as buying for a new puppy. It does not establish that every product needs every facet. Use a small assortment with enough variation for the chosen filters to produce useful results.

### zooplus

The [dry-food listing](https://www.zooplus.co.uk/shop/dogs/dry_dog_food) exposes both total-price and unit-price sorting. Entries include package size, price per kilogram, ratings, product options, purchase actions, and repeat-order promotion.

This is a useful reference for demonstrating transparent comparison between different pack sizes. Unit pricing requires consistent arithmetic and units in the fictional dataset. A subscription program would introduce a separate behavior decision; it is not necessary to adopt it to learn from the listing.

### Petstock

The [homepage](https://www.petstock.com.au/) presents seasonal product campaigns and named benefit areas for collection, delivery, rewards, and recurring purchases. It also includes many services. These are observed content propositions; fulfillment speed and service quality were not tested.

For a Brazilian retail showcase, the transferable idea is merchandising for a relevant occasion, rather than copying Australian seasonality or real collection promises. The main listing and purchase flow were not deeply inspected for this reference, so it should guide campaign/content discussion rather than checkout behavior.

### Heads Up For Tails

The [harness listing](https://headsupfortails.com/collections/harnesses) exposes facets for brand, life stage, breed size, color, price, and product type, plus quick-filter labels and product entries offering sizes. Its homepage groups everyday products alongside seasonal collections.

This provides an alternative to a purely utilitarian catalog: a theme can bring related products together without eliminating practical filtering. The trade-off is image and content workload. A named original collection needs consistent product relationships and a reason to exist, rather than decorative banners over arbitrary inventory.

### Cloud7

The [homepage](https://cloud7.de/en/) offers specialized fit pathways alongside product families and collections. The [Sleepy Deluxe Teddy product page](https://cloud7.de/en/products/dog-bed-sleepy-deluxe-teddy) includes material and care information.

The inspected [bed photograph](https://cloud7.de/cdn/shop/files/Cloud7-Dog-Bed-Sleepy-Deluxe-Teddy-Dog-2-on-grey.jpg?v=1754904624&width=900) places a light-colored dog in a textured bed against a light gray background. It communicates scale, texture, and use simultaneously. This suggests planning both isolated product views and in-use views; it does not imply the whole fictional store should adopt a neutral palette or premium positioning.

### Zee.Dog

The [international storefront](https://www.zeedog.com/) separates familiar product families from named collections and collaborations. Its extracted search content includes suggested popular searches, while homepage promotions express product use cases.

For an original store, category names can remain plain while a collection supplies personality. A product's benefit should be understandable without learning invented collection terminology. No branded collaboration, pattern, image, or slogan from this reference is recommended for reuse.

## Official app evidence and mobile implications

[Petlove's official app material](https://www.petlove.com.br/aplicativo?c=meuspedidos_greyhound&pid=site) describes managing recurring purchases and deliveries, offers, and pet-related reminders. Some retrieved material contains unresolved template content and older indexed copy; treat it as evidence of the publisher's described offering, not a current screen-by-screen audit.

The [Chewy App Store listing published by Chewy, Inc.](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chewy-pet-care-pharmacy/id1149449468) describes searching/shopping by category, deals, and recurring delivery. [Pets at Home's membership page](https://www.petsathome.com/pets-club) refers to using the website/app with a member account. These support studying discovery and repeat shopping as app concerns; they do not verify current tab bars, gestures, or checkout screens.

**Inference for later design discussion:** the complete demo path should remain understandable on a narrow screen, with usable product selection, filters, cart editing, and recovery from empty results. The visual prototype ticket should validate those tasks at mobile width. Do not assume an app-like bottom navigation or sticky purchase bar before comparing alternatives.

## Candidate aesthetic directions to discuss

These are original proposals derived from the research, **not descriptions of verified site-wide styling and not selected designs**.

| Candidate direction | Proposed character | Research inputs | Useful prototype question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Friendly everyday neighborhood store | Approachable typography, clear category choices, practical product information, pets in familiar home situations | Brazilian retail vocabulary; Chewy's discovery/support content | Can a local owner understand the offer and complete a purchase walkthrough quickly? |
| Expressive walk-and-play shop | Coordinated product families, original color combinations, one memorable collection, clear packshots | Wild One's kits; Zee.Dog's functional and collection pathways | Does the personality make products easier to recognize without obscuring price and selection? |
| Calm care-and-comfort boutique | Restrained composition, material detail, in-use imagery, concise suitability guidance | Cloud7's fit/material information and inspected bed image | Can a quieter direction still communicate affordable everyday essentials as well as premium pieces? |

A later prototype should compare these with the **same representative products and pt-BR content**, so the user reacts to design differences rather than unequal content quality. Palette, typefaces, exact density, illustration, and motion remain open.

## Candidate demonstration moments

The following are suggestions for the relevant decision tickets, not scope additions:

1. Find a suitable product by category and one meaningful attribute, with an understandable empty-result recovery.
2. Compare two fictional food pack sizes using consistent unit prices, if food enters the catalog.
3. Select an accessory size/color and see matching imagery and a precise cart description.
4. Discover an original small collection and understand how its items relate; a bundle is optional.
5. Edit quantity in the cart, see coherent totals, complete clearly simulated checkout, and restart the demonstration.

The first four draw on the catalog and product patterns above. The final item comes from the user's agreed demo scope; no retailer checkout was executed to validate it.

## Questions this evidence leaves for the map

The showcase-story decision must determine which moments help win over the intended clients. Catalog decisions must establish species, assortment, attributes, and fictional content. Brand and prototype discussions must select an original identity and visual direction. The image brief must decide roles, consistency, crop requirements, and sourcing using the separate image-source investigation. The demo-behavior decision must define cart and simulated checkout states.

Research is sufficient to compare these alternatives. Final design approval still needs rendered mobile/desktop prototypes and the user's reaction. No implementation, production-image library, or final DESIGN.md was created.
