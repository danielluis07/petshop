# Image sources for the fictional petshop showcase

Research date: 2026-10-26. All linked sources were accessed on that date.

Context: [Research image sources for an original fictional petshop](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/3), part of [Plan the fictional petshop showcase store](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/1).

## Finding

A coherent library is feasible through three routes: curated stock with a deliberately limited catalog, original/generated product imagery, or a mix of stock lifestyle photographs and original fictional products. The strongest candidate to discuss later is the mixed route: it separates the emotional job of pet photography from the accuracy and consistency required by product cards. This is a recommendation for comparison, not a chosen sourcing strategy or art direction.

The source license, the content depicted in an image, and permission to use an image as a generation reference are separate checks. The policies below are observations of the providers' published rules, not an assurance that any particular asset is cleared for this project.

## Compare the sourcing routes

The following assessments are project-specific inferences, not provider promises or measured production estimates.

| Route | Coherence and realism | Effort and dependency | Trade-off for this showcase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stock-led | Real animals and environments; matching lighting, crops, and product families requires curation. | Low production effort, potentially substantial searching and editing. Availability constrains the catalog. | Good for a smaller accessory-led catalog; difficult if many precise package/variant images are needed. |
| Original photography/artwork | Exact control over selected objects, packaging, and visual rules. | Requires objects or mockups, capture/design work, and repeatable setup. | Strong consistency and accurate variants, with the most direct production commitment. |
| Generated-led | Can depict invented products in one visual world. | Requires a tool, reference assets, iteration, and manual acceptance. Actual generation cost was not evaluated. | Flexible catalog, but output must be checked for product drift, unwanted marks, malformed anatomy, and packaging text. |
| Mixed | Real pet scenes plus a controlled product family can serve different roles well. | Requires two coordinated workflows and shared lighting/color/crop rules. | A useful balance if the chosen brand can make stock lifestyle and original product imagery feel related. |

Conditional shortlist for the later decision:

1. **Low production commitment:** curated free-stock lifestyle photographs plus a small set of generic accessory images that genuinely match the catalog. Let available usable imagery constrain product breadth.
2. **Distinctive fictional catalog:** curated lifestyle photographs plus original/generated packshots and deliberately designed fictional packaging. Approve one representative product family before extending it.
3. **Controlled production:** photograph or render a limited set of original props/packages, adding stock only where it fits. Consider paid matched stock if the selected brief cannot be met otherwise.

No route requires copying a retailer's product image. Retailer references and image licenses serve different purposes.

## Generation and fictional packaging

Adobe's official guidance distinguishes a [composition reference](https://helpx.adobe.com/firefly/web/work-with-images/generate-images/match-image-composition-to-reference-image.html), which guides arrangement, outline, and depth, from a [style reference](https://helpx.adobe.com/firefly/web/work-with-images/generate-images/reference-images-for-styling.html), which guides appearance. Its [generation guide](https://helpx.adobe.com/firefly/web/work-with-images/generate-images/generate-images-from-text-descriptions.html) also states that available settings vary by selected model. These controls are relevant examples; the report does not select Adobe or establish that an existing tool account supports a particular workflow.

For any generated route, propose an approved master view for each product family, then derive and compare related views against it. Record the intended product silhouette, material, colors, size, markings, and variant differences independently of the image prompt. Reject an image if it contradicts the catalog, even when it looks attractive. Treat apparent text on packaging as material that needs deliberate review; an original label can be designed and applied separately during later asset production.

Do not upload a retailer image merely because it is a useful visual reference. Adobe's [generative AI user guidelines](https://www.adobe.com/legal/licenses-terms/adobe-gen-ai-user-guidelines.html) prohibit infringement through inputs or outputs; its [reference-image tutorial](https://helpx.adobe.com/au/firefly/how-to/generate-image-using-reference-image.html) explicitly calls for the necessary rights to uploaded references. A license allowing a stock image's display does not by itself settle every proposed AI workflow. Select and record rights-cleared references for the actual production use.

Adobe states that it automatically attaches Content Credentials when all pixels are generated with Firefly, documenting origin-related information. Its user guidelines prohibit removing, altering, or disabling those credentials. Preserve them and the original master when planning web derivatives; verify the export workflow against those rules. Provenance metadata does not itself prove asset clearance. See [Adobe Content Credentials overview](https://helpx.adobe.com/firefly/web/get-started/learn-the-basics/content-credentials-overview.html) and [Adobe generative AI user guidelines, section 6](https://www.adobe.com/legal/licenses-terms/adobe-gen-ai-user-guidelines.html).

## What the later image brief must settle

The [image art-direction decision](https://github.com/danielluis07/petshop/issues/10) already owns these choices; no additional ticket is required solely because this report lists them.

| Brief field | Decision needed |
| --- | --- |
| Role and coverage | Which assets serve hero/storytelling, pet-category discovery, product lists, detail galleries, cart thumbnails, and any illustration? Count unique masters separately from crops/derivatives. |
| Product relationship | Identify the product and variant each packshot depicts. Decide which views can be shared and where sharing would misrepresent a variant. |
| Sourcing | Assign stock, generated, original photograph/artwork, or mixed production to each role. Identify paid options separately. |
| Composition | Agree focal subject, subject scale, orientation, camera angle, negative space, visual weight, and the treatment of human presence. |
| Art direction | Agree background, lighting direction/softness, shadows, color treatment, environments, props, and packaging rules across the whole library. |
| Ratios and responsive crops | Choose ratios from the approved layouts. A square card or a wide hero is a candidate, not a settled requirement. Specify mobile focal points and when a separate composition is needed. |
| Dimensions and delivery | Agree required display sizes, export dimensions/formats, transparency, compression expectations, and local delivery/hosting constraints after the app-structure decision. |
| Text and authenticity | Decide what packaging text is actually legible, how fictional product/brand markings stay consistent, and how imagery avoids suggesting real certification or retailer affiliation. |
| Acceptance | Inspect a representative family across card, detail, cart, and narrow-screen views before producing the remainder. Check catalog correspondence as well as visual quality. |
| Accessibility | Write contextual pt-BR alt text for informative imagery; distinguish decorative images. Describe the selected product/variant without relying on text baked into a bitmap. |
| Failure and missing assets | Specify a deliberate placeholder treatment and whether incomplete product imagery prevents that product from entering the demo catalog. |
| Provenance | Keep an asset ID, role, product/variant IDs, creator/source URL, license and access date, purchase/permission record if any, original master, edits, and approved derivatives. For generation, add provider/model, prompt/settings, reference IDs and rights, and selected output. |

Quantity should follow the chosen catalog and page compositions. This research sets neither a final image count nor a preferred palette, photographic mood, or illustration style.

## Limits and next decision

This was a public-source investigation. No provider account, generation model, license purchase, export pipeline, or visual asset family was tested. Published permissions vary by collection and feature and should be rechecked against the actual asset and intended use when production begins. Provider statements about commercial use have been reported without interpreting them as broad legal clearance.

The next image decision is to choose a sourcing mix and brief after the visual direction and catalog are agreed. A representative image-family trial may then become a precisely scoped prototype question if the selected route's consistency remains uncertain. It is currently too early to choose that trial's products or appearance.
